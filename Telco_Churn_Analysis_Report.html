Executive Summary

This report presents a data-driven analysis of customer churn across IBM's fictional Telco dataset covering 7,043 customers in California. With an overall churn rate of 26.5%, the business is losing roughly one in four customers — representing a critical revenue and growth risk.

The analysis reveals that churn is not random. It is concentrated within identifiable, predictable customer segments. Contract type, tenure, internet service type, payment method, and the absence of value-added services emerge as the most powerful predictors of churn. These findings create a clear roadmap for targeted, proactive retention interventions.

Specifically, month-to-month contract holders churn at 42.7% — more than 15× the rate of two-year contract customers. Fiber optic subscribers paying high monthly charges within their first 12 months represent the single highest-risk cohort. Addressing this segment alone could meaningfully reduce overall churn and protect monthly recurring revenue.

Key Churn Driver #2 — Customer Tenure

Churn Rate by Tenure Band (Months)
Early-lifecycle customers are disproportionately at risk — churn drops sharply after 24 months
Key Finding: Nearly 47.7% of customers who have been with the company for 0–12 months will churn. By contrast, customers who stay beyond 48 months churn at only 7%. The first year is the critical retention window — intervention at onboarding is most impactful.

Key Finding: Fiber optic customers — who pay significantly higher monthly charges — churn at 41.9%. This suggests a perceived value gap: customers pay premium prices but don't feel they receive premium value. Adding Online Security reduces churn from 41.8% to 14.6%.

Key Churn Driver #4 — Payment Method

Churn Rate by Payment Method
Electronic check users show markedly higher churn propensity
Key Finding: Customers paying by electronic check churn at 45.3% — nearly 3× the rate of credit card users (15.2%). Manual payment methods act as a proxy for lower engagement and commitment, making these customers prime candidates for auto-pay migration campaigns.

Key Churn Driver #5 — Demographics

Senior Citizen Churn

Senior citizens churn at 41.7% compared to 23.7% for non-seniors — reflecting potentially lower digital literacy, less awareness of migration process complexity, or more sensitivity to price increases. Customers without a partner or dependents also churn significantly more, suggesting that household switching costs (bundled plans, shared accounts) act as natural retention anchors. The business should consider family and group plan incentives as a structural retention mechanism.

Retention Strategies

📋

Contract Migration Campaign

Offer month-to-month customers a discounted rate (10–15%) to switch to 1-year or 2-year contracts. Use predictive scores to prioritise high-risk accounts first. Consider a 30-day free trial of annual benefits.

🎯 Target: M2M Customers · Expected Lift: High
🛡️

Security & Support Bundle

Bundle Online Security + Tech Support at no extra cost for the first 3 months for new Fiber optic customers. This closes the perceived value gap and reduces churn from ~42% to ~15% in the segment.

🎯 Target: Fiber Optic New Customers · Expected Lift: Very High
💳

Auto-Pay Incentive

Offer $5/month bill credit for customers who switch from electronic check to auto-pay (bank transfer or credit card). Reduces payment friction and correlates with 30-point lower churn rate.

🎯 Target: E-Check Users · Expected Lift: Medium
🌱

30/60/90 Day Onboarding

Deploy a structured check-in programme for all new customers: welcome call at day 7, satisfaction survey at day 30, personalised plan review at day 60, loyalty reward at day 90. Addresses the early-lifecycle churn cliff.

🎯 Target: Tenure <12 Months · Expected Lift: High
👴

Senior Care Programme

Dedicated support line for senior customers, simplified billing, in-home tech assistance, and loyalty pricing that doesn't increase with market adjustments. Pair with a "family plan" referral incentive.

🎯 Target: Senior Citizens · Expected Lift: Medium
📊

Churn Prediction Score

Deploy a real-time ML churn propensity score (gradient boosted classifier ~80% accuracy) for every customer. Flag high-risk accounts weekly in CRM for proactive outreach before they initiate cancellation.

🎯 Target: All Segments · Expected Lift: Highest ROI

Conclusions & Actionable Recommendations

1. Fix the first 12 months. The early-lifecycle churn rate (~47.7%) is the single biggest structural vulnerability. Implement a mandatory 30/60/90-day onboarding programme and assign a retention specialist to all new fiber optic customers within their first month.

2. Convert month-to-month customers. Launch a targeted contract upgrade campaign with pricing incentives. Even converting 20% of the month-to-month base to annual contracts could reduce overall churn by ~4–5 percentage points.

3. Bundle value-added services immediately. Online Security and Tech Support are proven churn-reducers. Offer them free for 3 months to new customers — the retention gain far outweighs the marginal service cost.

4. Deploy a churn propensity model in CRM. A gradient-boosted ML model on this dataset achieves ~80% accuracy. Scoring all 7,000+ customers weekly and alerting the retention team to any account crossing a 70% risk threshold is the highest-ROI action available.

5. Migrate e-check users to auto-pay. A small monthly bill credit to incentivise auto-pay adoption reduces both churn risk and payment processing costs simultaneously.

6. Design a Senior Customer programme. Senior citizens churn at nearly twice the base rate. Dedicated service channels, simplified onboarding, and loyalty pricing for this cohort will have outsized impact relative to effort.
